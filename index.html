Notchwave Privacy Policy

App: Notchwave for WindowsDeveloper: Som Anand SahooEffective date: 14 November 2026
Contact: somanandsahoo96@gmail.com

Notchwave is a Windows app that shows music, notifications, timers and other information in a small notch at the top of your screen. This policy explains what Notchwave accesses, where that data goes, how long it is kept and how you can delete it.

In short

  • No account is required. There is no sign-in.
  • No ads, no analytics, no telemetry. The developer does not collect or store any of your data. The one service the developer runs is a small relay that helps your PC and a phone set up a screen-sharing connection; it keeps no logs and forgets each connection when it ends (see Screen sharing).
  • No data is sold or shared with anyone for advertising or any other purpose.
  • Almost everything happens only on your PC. Notchwave goes online only for features you use (weather, your calendar link, synced lyrics, screen sharing over the internet), each described below.

Screen sharing

Screen sharing starts only when you start it, and nobody sees anything until they are allowed. Your screen is never recorded or saved, and never uploaded to the developer (it never passes through the relay): it exists only as a live, encrypted stream to the viewers you allow, while you share. By default the person who scanned the code taps Allow on their phone, as with WhatsApp Web (Settings → Screen sharing → Who approves can also, or instead, ask you on the PC; if you updated from version 1.0.0, it stays on the PC, as before). Both screens show the same match code. There are two ways to connect (Settings → Screen sharing → Connection):

  • Anywhere (the default; needs internet, nothing to set up). Phones on mobile data or another Wi-Fi can watch. Only the messages that set up the call pass through a relay: by default Notchwave's own relay, which the developer runs on Cloudflare (Cloudflare Workers, at notchwave-signal.notchwave-cloud.workers.dev, with the viewer page at notchwave-viewer.notchwave-cloud.workers.dev). Under Settings → Screen sharing → Advanced you can instead use a relay you deploy on your own free Cloudflare account; then nothing goes to the developer's relay at all. Those messages are: an identifier made from the one-time code (a hash, not the code itself); the phone's browser and system as its browser reports them (for example "Chrome on Android"); your PC's name; both devices' security-certificate fingerprints and the numbers behind the match code; the network addresses (local and public IP addresses) the two devices need to reach each other; Allow or Decline; whether the phone says it is on mobile data; and basic connection quality figures. The relay only passes them between your PC and the phone: it doesn't store them, and the developer doesn't look at them. The phone opens the viewer page from the same Cloudflare account as the relay, which receives the one-time code in the page address (the certificate check after the "#" never leaves the phone). The relay keeps nothing but each room's bookkeeping (when it started, a hash of the PC's key, counters) and, while a phone is connected, that phone's device type on its connection; all of it is deleted when the room ends (at most 4 hours). Neither the relay nor the viewer page keeps logs: Cloudflare's request and console logging is turned off for both. To stop strangers from using it up, the relay counts how many rooms and connections each IP address opened in the last few minutes; those counts are kept in memory only, never written down, and forgotten soon after. Only the viewer page's own site can connect phones to it, and your PC's key for each room travels inside the encrypted connection, never in a web address. Cloudflare hosts both, and its own privacy policy applies to its service.
  • Your screen, sound and microphone never go through the relay: they go straight between your PC and the phone, encrypted end to end (WebRTC, DTLS-SRTP), and the phone refuses a PC whose certificate doesn't match the code it scanned. Google's public STUN server helps the two devices find each other and sees only their IP addresses. Notchwave's relay doesn't use TURN, so the video goes directly or not at all. Only if you set up TURN on a relay of your own and a network blocks direct connections does the encrypted video pass through it, without being decrypted.
  • Same Wi-Fi only. Nothing goes over the internet: viewers on your own local network connect directly to your PC, which serves the page itself, and the video stays on your network. It is off on networks Windows marks as Public unless you allow them.

Either way, viewers can only watch, never control your PC; the viewer page stores nothing on the phone (no cookies, no saved data); the notch and your notifications are kept out of the picture (on by default); and sharing stops by itself after a time limit you set, when the PC locks or sleeps, and when nobody has watched for 5 minutes. The sharing page runs in Microsoft Edge WebView2, a Windows component loaded only while you share; Microsoft's own terms apply to it.

Sharing with third parties

Notchwave does not sell, rent or share your personal data. The only data that reaches a third party is what you ask for through the internet features above, and the call set-up of a screen share, which passes through Cloudflare, the host of Notchwave's sharing relay.

Children

Notchwave is a general-purpose utility. It does not knowingly collect personal information from anyone, including children under 13, and it has no accounts, chat or ads.

Your choices

  • Turn any feature off in Settings → Features.
  • Withdraw notification, camera, microphone or Bluetooth access at any time in Windows Settings → Privacy & security.
  • Delete everything by uninstalling Notchwave. For versions not from the Store, also delete %LOCALAPPDATA%\Notchwave (and %APPDATA%\DynamicIsland if an older version left it).
